What counts as a delay Lead time is how long the order took. Delay is the gap between what was expected and what happened. A 412-day open tender is a long lead time but not a delay if 412 days were expected; a 70-day order expected in 60 is a 10-day delay.

What each metric means

What is measuredApplies toDefinition and caveat
AccuracyForecastTotal error compared against total consumption, at item level, weighted by value. Measured at item level and weighted by value.
Average errorForecastSum of absolute errors divided by sum of actuals. Measured as total error over total consumption, because a simple percentage breaks down when consumption in a month is zero — which is most months for an intermittent item.
Over- or under-estimateForecastThe average of the over- and under-estimates. A positive figure means the estimate runs high, so stock builds up.
Average error, in per cent and daysLead timeError on predicted PR-to-GRN duration, in per cent and in days.
Arrivals within the predicted rangeLead timeShare of orders where the actual GRN date fell inside the predicted range. Sits near the stated confidence level, not near 100%.
Alerts that were rightAlert engineOf the items flagged, the share that turned out to matter. If this runs low, users stop trusting the list.
Events caught in advanceAlert engineOf the events that actually occurred, the share flagged in advance. Depends on NTPC's stock-out records being complete — an event definition is still to be agreed.
Override rateAll calculated figuresShare of calculated figures flagged by IMG with a reason. Not a failure measure — it is the feedback channel.
