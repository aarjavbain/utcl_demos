Category Strategy

The forged-vs-chrome spec decision, tested savings levers and the wider lever universe across the ~$101M base
Forged vs. high-chrome — the headline spec lever
strategic

The headline example is Plant 3: switching ~$14M of forged spend to high-chrome is modelled at ~42% / ~$6M by the Entity B team — the single largest spend-better prize in the category. The logic generalises: high-chrome costs more per tonne and carries a thinner supplier margin (~10% vs ~27% forged), but in abrasive ores it wears far slower, so fewer tonnes of media are consumed per tonne of ore. With ~75% of volume still forged, even a partial, ore-specific shift — Plant 2 is the next candidate at ~$15M / ~3% — is a large prize, gated by Technical Services trials and de-risked with supplier performance guarantees. Value sized by soliciting wear-rate data through the RFP.

Structural constraints
1

Local-content rules limit consolidation (Market 5)

Market 5 requires locally-sourced grinding media, so a single global supplier cannot serve all sites. Ability to pool volume is constrained at the remaining Market 5 site (Plant 7); Plant 12 has since been divested.

Hybrid award — consolidate & grow share with global suppliers to create regional competition, award locally where mandated and benchmark against global references.

2

Spec switching needs trials & Technical Services sign-off

Switching specifications (e.g. forged→high-chrome, ball mix) requires extensive trial periods and alignment with sites and Technical Services. Optimal award scenarios can be constrained by these approvals.

Performance guarantees underwritten by suppliers so Entity A can trial specs without financial loss; build trial volumes and contract flexibility into the RFP.

3

Raw material is 64–86% of price & index-linked

Pricing is closely tied to regional steel indices, electricity and logistics, limiting absolute price flexibility. Steel prices reset sharply higher in H1 2026 and have since held at that elevated level.

Negotiate a clear weighted rise/fall formula on the lowest-cost regional index, with explicit price-decrease language and bi-annual resets to limit volatility.

4

Site volume volatility (−46% to +109% YoY)

Individual site volumes swing sharply year-on-year even though combined volume is far steadier, driven by stock-holding and excess inventory.

Strategic regional bundling so total contract value absorbs site peaks/troughs and reduces supplier visibility to volatility; standardised rise/fall mechanisms.

Market Intelligence

Grinding-media & steel market signals mapped to Entity A’s category levers
Market Briefing| September 2026AI-Augmented

The grinding-media market is still a buyer’s market — global capacity (~9–10 MMT) continues to run ahead of demand — but the cost base has moved. Steel prices surged over 10% in Q1 2026 on geopolitical disruption and tariff action, and have since settled into an elevated “new normal” rather than reversing. Set against that, India cement demand is growing ~6–7% in 2026, well ahead of the global average, and domestic ferrochrome supply gives Indian high-chrome buyers a structural landed-cost edge over importers.

Analysis

The window still favours the buyer, but the reference price has reset higher: run the RFP against the current should-cost floor (forged ~$930/MT, high-chrome ~$1,195/MT in China) rather than last year’s numbers, and lock a weighted rise/fall formula on the lowest-cost regional index before the next reset. Two India-specific factors sharpen the case — growing domestic demand supports deeper volume-rebate tiers, and local ferrochrome access should be tested as a should-cost input rather than assumed away. Buyer power is high everywhere except Market 5, where local-content rules require a hybrid award.

Cost structure
Should-cost · 3 weeks ago

China raw-material cost basis remains structurally lower than other regions

Raw materials still run a meaningfully lower share of total cost in China than in North America or Europe — the reference point for should-cost negotiations, alongside the domestic ferrochrome comparison for Indian buyers.

Raw-material share of cost by region
should-cost

Raw material dominates the cost structure everywhere, but its share — and therefore exposure to steel-index moves — is highest in North America (~86%) and lowest in China (~64%). That ~17-point gap is why sourcing steel/chrome from low-cost regions and negotiating the right index base is the single biggest should-cost lever. China also runs the thinnest average margin (5–15%) vs 15–20% in NA/Europe.

Porter's Five Forces — grinding media

The structure favours Entity A as a buyer: a fragmented supplier base, multiple sourcing options in every region except Market 5, low switching barriers and an over-supplied market all push buyer power high. The mining downturn and weak commodity prices add further leverage. The main checks are Market 5's limited local-supplier pool (local content) and rising rivalry as suppliers expand APAC capacity — both arguments for a global tender that creates competitive tension while preserving local awards where mandated.

Supplier intelligence & competitive tension
benchmarked

The leverage in this category comes from a simple structural fact: every major position has a credible challenger. On forged, Supplier 1’s big incumbencies are under live attack from a hungrier, often tariff-advantaged Supplier 2; on high-chrome, Supplier 3 and Supplier 4 are near-mirror specialists trading sites at low-single-digit TCO gaps. A global RFP that pits incumbents against challengers — and lets high-chrome bids (Supplier 4/Supplier 3) contest forged incumbencies via the spec switch — is what converts that tension into price. Patterns below are benchmarked from a recent global mining-sector grinding-media RFP; supplier roles are general category dynamics.
